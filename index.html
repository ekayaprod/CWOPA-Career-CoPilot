How the Workflow Goes

Here is how to go from your past work history to a completed state application, one step at a time.

  1. 1

    Tell the AI About Your Past Work

    After pasting the Job Helper Prompt into your chat, upload your resume or describe your past jobs in plain sentences. The AI translates your everyday tasks into state hiring terms and suggests which Job Categories to check on the PA portal.

  2. 2

    Find & Compare Job Openings

    Filter the PA State Jobs Portal ↗ using those categories, then copy and paste postings into your chat so the AI can check the minimum requirements and help you pick the best opening.

    Computer shortcut: Click PA Jobs Bookmark on a search results page to scan the whole list at once.

  3. 3

    Answer the Job Questions

    Open the job posting you want to apply for and share both the posting and its Job Questions (found on the posting's Questions tab or on the application's questions step). On many postings, your answers to these questions determine whether you qualify and how your application is scored. The AI asks you follow-up questions to pull out real details from your past work so your answers earn the highest score you honestly qualify for.

    Computer shortcut: Click PA Jobs Bookmark on the job posting or application questions page to copy all questions at once.

  4. 4

    Check & Fill Out Your Application

    Read over every drafted answer in your chat to make sure it is accurate, then copy and paste your approved answers into the application form.

    Computer shortcut (using the auto-fill code at the end of your chat):
    1. On the application's Questions page, click PA Jobs Bookmark and choose Auto-Fill Answers.
    2. Paste the auto-fill code and click Check Answers (Recommended) to verify character limits and required fields.
    3. Click Approve & Fill Form.

See How "Creative Pivots" Work

In your chat, the AI groups illustrative openings into Direct Fits and Creative Pivots (state jobs with unfamiliar titles whose day-to-day work matches skills you already have), without adding or exaggerating a single fact.
